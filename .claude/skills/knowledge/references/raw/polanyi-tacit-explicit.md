# Michael Polanyi — Tacit vs. Explicit Knowledge

**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacit_knowledge
**Retrieved:** 2026-04-19
**Citation:** Polanyi, Michael. 1966. *The Tacit Dimension*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press.

## Core formulation

> "We can know more than we can tell." — Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension* (1966)

The statement asserts that knowledge extends beyond what language can capture
or communicate.

## Tacit knowledge

- Comprises skills, ideas, and experiences people possess but cannot easily codify
  or articulate.
- People are often unaware of the tacit knowledge they hold.
- Rooted in embodied experience — residing in bodies and minds as learned
  capabilities.
- Examples: recognising a face without being able to describe distinguishing
  features, riding a bicycle, mastering a surgical technique.
- Develops through practice and direct experience, not formal instruction.

## Explicit knowledge

- Formalised, codified, readily transferable through language, writing, or visual
  representation.
- Example: "London is in the United Kingdom" — can be written down, communicated
  precisely, and understood universally without personal interaction.

## Why the distinction matters

### For knowledge transfer

- Explicit knowledge travels easily — documents, manuals, databases.
- Tacit knowledge requires "extensive personal contact, regular interaction, and
  trust" — apprenticeships, mentorship, laboratory visits.

### For codification

- Not all tacit knowledge can be converted to explicit form.
- Some tacit knowledge is externalisable through effort (Nonaka–Takeuchi), some
  remains fundamentally resistant — especially collective tacit knowledge
  embedded in social structures or somatic knowledge in physical capability.

### For organisations

- Explains why documentation alone fails to transfer expertise. Bessemer patented
  his steelmaking process but could not convey enough knowledge for others to
  replicate his success; his tacit understanding remained locked in practice.

### Polanyi's deeper claim

All knowledge is ultimately rooted in tacit dimensions — even explicit formulations
depend on unspoken, learned capacities to interpret them.

## Relevance to the skills framework

- An AI model can only consume **explicit** knowledge. Tacit knowledge must be
  externalised (Nonaka's SECI) before it becomes useful to the model.
- Our `knowledge/` folder holds explicit knowledge directly.
- Our `experience/` folder holds **externalised tacit knowledge** — rules,
  heuristics, and patterns that were once tacit practice, crystallised into
  explicit, codified form for the model to consume.
- Polanyi's insight warns us: some practitioner expertise cannot be fully
  codified; we should flag such gaps honestly rather than pretend completeness.
