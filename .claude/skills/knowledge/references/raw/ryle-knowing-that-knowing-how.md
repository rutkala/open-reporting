# Gilbert Ryle — Knowing That vs. Knowing How

**Source:** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/knowledge-how/ (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)
**Retrieved:** 2026-04-19
**Citation:** Ryle, Gilbert. 1949. *The Concept of Mind*. London: Hutchinson.

## The distinction

Ryle's *The Concept of Mind* (1949) challenged the "intellectualist legend" that
all knowledge reduces to propositional knowledge. He argued knowledge comes in
two fundamentally different forms:

- **Knowing that** — propositional content about the world. Example: "Ithaca is in
  New York State." Acquired through being told, often suddenly. Not gradable.
- **Knowing how** — practical abilities and skilled performance. Example: knowing
  how to play piano, make pie, walk, or speak. Acquired gradually through practice
  and inculcation. **Gradable**: one can know how "in part" or "better than someone
  else".

## The regress argument

If knowing how reduced to knowing various propositions about how to perform tasks,
performing any action intelligently would require first contemplating an infinite
chain of progressively more complex propositions — an impossibility. Therefore
knowing how must be something fundamentally different from propositional knowledge.

## The contested question

Ryle's position is called **anti-intellectualism**: knowing how and knowing that
are distinct, irreducible forms of knowledge; knowing how consists of abilities or
intelligent dispositions to act.

Modern **intellectualists** argue Ryle was mistaken and that knowing how ultimately
reduces to a specific form of propositional knowledge — practical knowledge of
ways to perform tasks. The debate remains open in contemporary epistemology.

## Relevance to the skills framework

- ESCO's distinction between *knowledge* and *skill/competence* maps Ryle's
  *knowing that* / *knowing how* onto an occupational framework.
- In our skill model: `knowledge/` holds *knowing that* (propositional, declarative);
  `experience/` holds crystallised *knowing how* (procedural, codified through
  practice); `assets/` is the applied output — the skill in Ryle's sense.
- An AI model's training ingests propositional content. Practical abilities ("knowing
  how") are only available to it to the extent they are codified into propositional
  form — which is precisely what our `experience/` folder does.
