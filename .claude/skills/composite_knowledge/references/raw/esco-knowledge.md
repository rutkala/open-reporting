# ESCO — Definition of Knowledge (Escopedia entry)

**Source:** https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/about-esco/escopedia/escopedia/knowledge
**Retrieved:** 2026-04-19

## Primary definition (verbatim)

> "Knowledge means the outcome of the assimilation of information through
> learning. Knowledge is the body of facts, principles, theories and practices
> that is related to a field of work or study."

The definition is adopted directly from the European Qualifications Framework (EQF).

## Distinction from skills and competences

> "The difference lies in the way this knowledge is applied and being put into use."

- Knowledge = the content (facts, principles, theories, practices)
- Skills and competences = ways knowledge is deployed in action
- Both skills and competences rely on knowledge as their foundation

## Structure within ESCO v1

- Knowledge exists within the **skills pillar**.
- Knowledge concepts connect to skills/competences through qualified relations:
  - **Essential** — knowledge required for the skill
  - **Optional** — knowledge that supports but is not required for the skill
- Example (from ESCO): *procedural law* is **essential** for pleading a case in
  court; *labour law* is **optional**.

## Classification

Knowledge concepts are classified using:

> ISCED Fields of Education and Training 2013 (ISCED-F 2013)

## Formulation rules

- Knowledge terms avoid action verbs.
- No use of "know" or "knowledge" in the term itself.
- Example: use *"fermentation process"* rather than *"know fermentation process"*.

## Relevance to the skills framework

- ESCO gives us the institutional, policy-grade definition of knowledge:
  assimilation of information into a body of facts, principles, theories, and
  practices tied to a field.
- The **application-based distinction** from skills/competences is the cornerstone
  of our three-layer model: `knowledge/` (content) / `experience/` (crystallised
  procedural knowledge) / `assets/` (the applied output, the skill in use).
- The **essential vs. optional** relation is useful terminology when we later
  link a skill to the specific knowledge required to perform it.
