# Bloom's Revised Taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl 2001)

## Two dimensions

The 2001 revision replaced Bloom's original single hierarchy (1956) with a
**two-dimensional matrix**:

- **Knowledge Dimension** — *what* is known
- **Cognitive Process Dimension** — *what is done with* what is known

Each educational objective becomes a cell in the matrix (type of knowledge × cognitive process).

## Knowledge Dimension — four types

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Factual** | Basic elements to be acquainted with a discipline — terminology, specific details and elements | Technical vocabulary, key events, major figures, important dates |
| **Conceptual** | Interrelationships among basic elements within a larger structure — classifications, categories, principles, generalisations, theories, models | The periodic table as a classification system; Newton's laws as principles; macroeconomic models |
| **Procedural** | How to do something — methods of inquiry, subject-specific skills, algorithms, techniques, and criteria for using them | How to compute a standard deviation; how to construct a literature review; when to use interviews vs. surveys |
| **Metacognitive** | Knowledge of cognition in general as well as awareness and knowledge of one's own cognition | Strategic knowledge; self-knowledge of learning strengths and weaknesses; knowledge about when and why to use particular strategies |

## Cognitive Process Dimension — six levels (verbatim from source)

| Level | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | "Recognizing or recalling facts, terms, basic concepts, or answers" |
| **Understand** | "Demonstrating an understanding of facts and ideas by organizing and summarizing information" |
| **Apply** | "Using acquired knowledge to solve problems in new or unfamiliar situations" |
| **Analyze** | "Breaking down information into parts to understand relationships, motives, or causes" |
| **Evaluate** | "Making judgments about information, based on set criteria or standards" |
| **Create** | Combining elements to produce new meaning (highest level) |

## Key differences from the original 1956 taxonomy

- **1956 categories:** Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, Evaluation.
- **2001 categories:** Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create.
- The top two were **renamed and reordered** — Synthesis became Create and moved
  to the top above Evaluation.
- The taxonomy was "restructured on more systematic lines", addressing criticisms
  of the original's lack of systematic rationale.
- The addition of a separate **Knowledge Dimension** is the most important change:
  it separated *what is known* from *what is done with it*, making the framework
  two-dimensional.

## Relevance to the skills framework

- The **Knowledge Dimension's four types** give our `knowledge/` bucket a
  principled taxonomy: factual, conceptual, procedural, metacognitive.
- **Procedural knowledge** in Bloom's taxonomy is what our `experience/` folder
  largely contains — externalised procedures, heuristics, and criteria for use.
- **Metacognitive knowledge** is what this file itself represents — knowledge
  *about* knowledge, at the skill-framework level.
- The **Cognitive Process Dimension** describes what the skill's consumer
  (an AI model or a human) will do with the knowledge: the skill must support
  the full range — Remember (find the file), Apply (use it in context),
  Create (produce new artifacts).
