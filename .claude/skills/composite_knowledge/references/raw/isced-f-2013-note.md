Wikipedia's general ISCED article does not detail ISCED-F 2013's broad fields. The summary below reflects what was confirmed; specific field names are a known gap.

## What is known

- **Authority:** UNESCO Institute for Statistics
- **Publication year of ISCED-F:** 2013 (formally adopted)
- **Classification of:** education **fields / subject areas** (not levels).
  ISCED-P classifies levels of education (0–8); ISCED-F classifies fields of study.
- **Status:** "Independent but related" to ISCED-P — separate process, parallel use.
- **Use by ESCO:** ESCO's knowledge pillar is classified using ISCED-F 2013.
  (source: https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/about-esco/escopedia/escopedia/knowledge)

## Gap

The 11 broad fields (codes 00–10) and their names have not been fetched into this
raw folder. They are not load-bearing for the definitional synthesis — they are
operational infrastructure. If a future skill needs to classify its knowledge
domain against the ISCED-F taxonomy, the first step is to retrieve the UNESCO
manual directly.

## Relevance to the skills framework

- ISCED-F 2013 is an example of a **domain classification** — a way to locate a
  skill's knowledge in the universe of all fields.
- Our skill framework does not mandate ISCED-F classification; each skill can
  describe its field in plain language. We keep ISCED-F as a reference for
  cases that need formal alignment with European qualifications systems.
